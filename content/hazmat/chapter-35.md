## Overview

This chapter covers HazMat protective clothing, respiratory protection, PPE selection, chemical resistance, PPE limitations, inspection and maintenance, and PPE contamination and decontamination.

## Quick notes

### PPE Protection Levels

- Level A: Highest listed skin, eye, and respiratory protection level in the EPA classification system.
- Level B: High respiratory protection with a lesser degree of skin protection than Level A.
- Level C: Chemical protective clothing with an appropriate air-purifying respirator where conditions permit.
- Level D: Basic work clothing; minimal chemical protection.

### Important Definitions

- Degradation: Material damage or change caused by chemical contact.
- Penetration: Chemicals passing through openings or closures.
- Permeation: Chemicals passing through protective material at the molecular level.

## Key terms

- **Level A** — Highest listed skin, eye, and respiratory protection level in the EPA classification system.
- **Level B** — High respiratory protection with a lesser degree of skin protection than Level A.
- **Level C** — Chemical protective clothing with an appropriate air-purifying respirator where conditions permit.
- **Level D** — Basic work clothing; minimal chemical protection.
- **Degradation** — Material damage or change caused by chemical contact.
- **Penetration** — Chemicals passing through openings or closures.
- **Permeation** — Chemicals passing through protective material at the molecular level.

## Exam takeaways

PPE must be selected for the specific hazard and task, within training and organizational procedures. No level of PPE guarantees unlimited or complete protection.
