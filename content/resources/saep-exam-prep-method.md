## What this page is

A summary, in this site's own words, of the method the book asks you to follow. The three examinations and their answer keys are on the site word for word; this page only explains how the book wants them used. Where the book's wording matters, read the printed pages listed above.

## The three phases

- **Phase I** is the three 150-item examinations (I-1, I-2, I-3), each drawn from the parts of NFPA 1001 for Firefighter I. Exam I-1 surveys weaknesses, I-2 adds difficulty and depth, and I-3 confirms what you have mastered. Each exam has a reading list from the IFSTA Essentials of Fire Fighting (8th edition) and the Jones and Bartlett Fundamentals of Fire Fighter Skills (5th edition).
- **Phase II** explains how professional exam writers build items: verbatim items from the reference text, items that need deduction, and items that need inference from tables, charts, or a set of facts. The book's point is that recognizing the technique helps you read the item correctly.
- **Phase III** covers the mental and physical side of test day. The book treats readiness as part of preparation, not an afterthought.

## How the book wants each exam taken

1. Take the exam cold. Do not study first; the exam is meant to find weak areas.
2. Mark answers in ink and do not change them. The book reports that most changed answers in its field tests went from right to wrong.
3. Mark every guessed item with an X, even when the guess turns out right. A guess means the content is not known yet.
4. Score the exam against Appendix A.
5. For every missed or guessed item, find the correct answer in the cited reference, highlight it, then read the paragraph before and after it. The book calls this learning by context and association and says it is where most of the gain comes from.
6. Only then move to the next exam.

On this site, Exam mode hides the answers until you submit, which matches step 1. Practice mode shows the book's answer and citations as you go, which matches step 5. The Mark for review and Guessed boxes stand in for the X.

## The book's rules

- Preparation is 95 percent perspiration and 5 percent inspiration.
- Follow the steps in order; do not shortcut the system.
- Mark and research every guess.
- Read a question twice when it has complex directions.
- Training and testing must be aligned to the job for either to work.
- Exam items should be written by subject-matter experts with current experience.
- Good luck equals good preparation.

## The book's hints

- Your first impression is usually right; think twice before changing an answer.
- Researching the correct answer, and the paragraphs around it, is the single most important activity.
- Work through every missed item the same way; retention comes from reading the whole paragraph.
- When you miss an item, work out why you chose the wrong answer and what separates it from the right one.
- The book's own field tests reported gains of about 17 points between the first and third 100-item exams, and more when 150-item exams were used.

## Scoring on the Personal Progress Plotter

The book's progress plotter records, for each exam, the number guessed and the number missed. Its exam score formula subtracts (guessed plus missed) times the point value per item from 150. On a 150-item exam each item is worth 0.67 points, so 13 guessed-or-missed items give 150 minus 13 times 0.67, about 91.3. The site's percentage score is simpler: correct divided by total. Treat guessed items as missed if you want to match the book's number.

## Target

After each exam the book asks whether you scored 80 percent or higher on the feedback step. If not, it sends you back to the highlighted answers for another pass through the surrounding paragraphs before the next exam. The site's pass and target markers (70 percent and 80 percent) follow the same idea.
