## Overview

This chapter covers fire chemistry, heat and energy, combustion, fire development, heat transfer, smoke behavior, flammable limits, fire classifications, and dangerous fire conditions.

## Quick notes

### Fire Science Basics

- Matter: Anything with mass and volume.
- Potential energy: Stored energy.
- Kinetic energy: Energy of motion.
- Oxidation: Reaction involving oxygen.
- Combustion: Rapid oxidation producing heat and usually light.
- Pyrolysis: Decomposition of material by heat.
- Autoignition: Ignition caused by heat without an external spark or flame.

### Fire Triangle

- Heat
- Fuel
- Oxygen

### Fire Tetrahedron

- Heat
- Fuel
- Oxygen
- Chemical chain reaction

### Three Methods of Heat Transfer

- Conduction: Heat transfer through contact.
- Convection: Heat transfer through moving gases or liquids.
- Radiation: Heat transfer through electromagnetic energy.

### Four Stages of Fire Development

1. Incipient: Early fire, localized to the initial fuel.
2. Growth: Heat release and fire spread increase.
3. Fully developed: Peak heat release rate in the compartment.
4. Decay: Fire decreases due to reduced available fuel or oxygen.

### Dangerous Fire Conditions

- Flashover: Rapid transition to widespread compartment involvement.
- Rollover: Ignition of accumulated combustible gases in the ceiling layer.
- Backdraft: Explosive ignition associated with air entering an oxygen-deficient fire compartment.
- Smoke explosion: Ignition of accumulated smoke in a separate area.

### Smoke Behavior

- Plume: Rising column of hot gases, flames, and smoke.
- Heat stratification: Gases forming layers according to temperature.
- Flow path: Movement of heat and smoke between areas of differing pressure.
- Neutral plane: Interface between outgoing hot gases and incoming cooler air at an opening.
- Laminar smoke flow: Smooth movement.
- Turbulent smoke flow: Agitated movement.
- Ventilation-limited fire: Fire growth limited by available oxygen.
- Fuel-limited fire: Fire growth limited by fuel characteristics or availability.

### Temperatures and Limits

- Flash point: Temperature where enough vapor is produced for momentary ignition.
- Fire point: Temperature supporting sustained combustion.
- Boiling point: Temperature where liquid vapor pressure equals surrounding pressure.
- Ignition temperature: Minimum temperature required for ignition under specified conditions.
- LEL: Lower Explosive Limit.
- UEL: Upper Explosive Limit.
- Flammable range: Concentrations between LEL and UEL.

### Five Fire Classes

- A: Ordinary combustible materials.
- B: Flammable liquids and gases.
- C: Energized electrical equipment.
- D: Combustible metals.
- K: Cooking oils and fats.

### Toxic Combustion Products

- Carbon monoxide: Toxic gas associated with incomplete combustion.
- Hydrogen cyanide: Highly toxic gas produced by combustion of certain materials.
- Smoke can contain hazardous gases and particulates.
