### CHAPTER 5 — FIRE BEHAVIOR
Status: Study Notes Available. Midterm Questions: 34–45
What This Chapter Covers: Fire chemistry; Heat and energy; Combustion; Fire development; Heat transfer; Smoke behavior; Flammable limits; Fire classifications; Dangerous fire conditions
Fire Science Basics:
- Matter: Anything with mass and volume.
- Potential energy: Stored energy.
- Kinetic energy: Energy of motion.
- Oxidation: Reaction involving oxygen.
- Combustion: Rapid oxidation producing heat and usually light.
- Pyrolysis: Decomposition of material by heat.
- Autoignition: Ignition caused by heat without an external spark or flame.
Fire Triangle: Heat, Fuel, Oxygen. Fire Tetrahedron: Heat, Fuel, Oxygen, Chemical chain reaction.
Three Methods of Heat Transfer: Conduction: Heat transfer through contact. Convection: Heat transfer through moving gases or liquids. Radiation: Heat transfer through electromagnetic energy.
Four Stages of Fire Development: 1. Incipient: Early fire, localized to the initial fuel. 2. Growth: Heat release and fire spread increase. 3. Fully developed: Peak heat release rate in the compartment. 4. Decay: Fire decreases due to reduced available fuel or oxygen.
Dangerous Fire Conditions: Flashover: Rapid transition to widespread compartment involvement. Rollover: Ignition of accumulated combustible gases in the ceiling layer. Backdraft: Explosive ignition associated with air entering an oxygen-deficient fire compartment. Smoke explosion: Ignition of accumulated smoke in a separate area.
Smoke Behavior: Plume: Rising column of hot gases, flames, and smoke. Heat stratification: Gases forming layers according to temperature. Flow path: Movement of heat and smoke between areas of differing pressure. Neutral plane: Interface between outgoing hot gases and incoming cooler air at an opening. Laminar smoke flow: Smooth movement. Turbulent smoke flow: Agitated movement. Ventilation-limited fire: Fire growth limited by available oxygen. Fuel-limited fire: Fire growth limited by fuel characteristics or availability.
Temperatures and Limits: Flash point: Temperature where enough vapor is produced for momentary ignition. Fire point: Temperature supporting sustained combustion. Boiling point: Temperature where liquid vapor pressure equals surrounding pressure. Ignition temperature: Minimum temperature required for ignition under specified conditions. LEL: Lower Explosive Limit. UEL: Upper Explosive Limit. Flammable range: Concentrations between LEL and UEL.
Five Fire Classes: A: Ordinary combustible materials. B: Flammable liquids and gases. C: Energized electrical equipment. D: Combustible metals. K: Cooking oils and fats.
Toxic Combustion Products: Carbon monoxide: Toxic gas associated with incomplete combustion. Hydrogen cyanide: Highly toxic gas produced by combustion of certain materials. Smoke can contain hazardous gases and particulates.
Key Takeaway: Understand how fires ignite, grow, transfer heat, move smoke, and create dangerous conditions.

### CHAPTER 35 — HAZARDOUS MATERIALS RESPONDER PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT

### CHAPTER 36 — PRODUCT CONTROL

Important Definitions: Confinement: Keeping released material within a defined area. Containment: Stopping material from escaping its container. Diking: Creating barriers to restrict liquid movement. ### 2. THE 2024 EMERGENCY RESPONSE GUIDEBOOK
Purpose: The ERG assists responders during the initial phase of hazardous materials transportation incidents.
ERG Sections: White: General instructions, safety, identification charts, and reference information. Yellow: Materials listed by four-digit UN/NA identification number. Blue: Materials listed alphabetically by name. Orange: Initial response guides, hazards, and emergency response information. Green: Initial isolation and protective action distance tables and related information.
Study Sequence: 1. Identify the material using reliable information. 2. Find the appropriate ERG entry and guide. 3. Read the appropriate orange response guide. 4. Use the green tables when indicated. 5. Follow incident command and the appropriate protective-action guidance.
Important Notes: ERG guidance is primarily for initial transportation incident response. It does not replace training or chemical-specific technical references. Do not invent isolation distances. Do not calculate protective action distances from memory. Use the actual guide tables and incident circumstances. The uploaded edition is ERG 2024.
Future Website Feature: Include a link to the official ERG resource and a study-mode guide explaining its organization. Do not build a simulated operational ERG lookup that invents chemical data or distances.

### 3. NFPA 704 HAZARD DIAMOND
Blue: Health. Red: Flammability. Yellow: Instability/reactivity. White: Special hazards. Show how numerical hazard ratings communicate severity. Also explain that NFPA 704 fixed-facility hazard identification is different from DOT transportation placards.
